## Nashville, IN Competitive Landscape

Despite being a well-known artist colony, there are very few structured retreat offerings in Nashville, IN. That's an opportunity.

Brown County has galleries, studios, artisan shops, and a long creative history — but no one is running an all-inclusive visual art retreat as a product.

**What currently exists in/near Nashville, IN:**

- **Waycross Artists, Writers & Readers Retreat** — Nov 2026, 2 nights, $280–$350/person. Lodging + meals + program fees. Self-directed (bring your own project, no formal instruction). Conference center setting.
- **Winter Wooly Retreat (Paige Shipley Designs)** — Feb 2026, 2 nights at Brown County Inn, ~$300–$400/person. Fiber arts specific (knitting). 90 attendees. Meals now optional. Classes + goodybag included.
- **SCBWI Spring Into Story** — May 2026, 2 nights, ~$280–$350/person. Writing-focused, limited to 8 writers. Lodging + meals + workshops at Brown County State Park.
- **Brown County Art Guild** — Single workshops, $165–$195/session. Instruction only, no retreat format.
- **Second Story Studio (Bloomington, ~30 min away)** — Drop-in workshops, no retreat format.

**The gap:** No structured, all-inclusive *visual art* retreat exists. The existing programs are either self-directed, writing/fiber-specific, or single-session workshops without the retreat wrapper.

## What $500 Buys in This Market

At $500/person, Nicole is positioned:
- **Above** the self-directed/venue-only options ($280–$400) — because she's offering instruction, curation, and a complete experience
- **In line with** Paper & i, DoArtWorx, and Art & Contemplation — established retreats with loyal followings
- **Below** the institutional programs (Arrowmont $811+, Shenandoah $895+) — which makes it approachable for people trying a retreat for the first time

This is the "I can say yes without agonizing" price point. It's a weekend away that feels special without feeling like a financial stretch. That's a powerful position for building a repeat audience.

## How to Make $500 Feel Like a Steal (Without Undercharging)

The retreats that thrive at this price point do it by being *thoughtful*, not expensive:

- **All-inclusive simplicity** — one price, everything's covered, no nickel-and-diming
- **Small group** — 8–12 people. Intimacy is the luxury, not the venue.
- **The setting does the work** — Nashville's artist colony reputation, Brown County's natural beauty, fall colors. You don't need a resort when the town itself is the draw.
- **Personal touches over expensive ones** — a handwritten welcome note costs nothing. Knowing everyone's name by dinner costs nothing. A curated playlist costs nothing.
- **Feed people well** — doesn't mean fancy. Means thoughtful. Good coffee, fresh fruit, a real dinner together.

The Winter Wooly Retreat charges $300–$400 for 90 people in a hotel conference room. Nicole can charge $500 for 8–12 people in an intimate setting with real instruction and it'll feel like a completely different (and better) experience.

## Regional Programs Worth Knowing About

These aren't direct competitors (they're hours away and mostly pricier), but they show what's out there and how Nicole's offering compares favorably.

**Shake Rag Alley** — Mineral Point, WI (pop. ~2,500)
- 250+ workshops per year, half-day to 6-day
- $50–$295 per workshop (instruction only, no lodging)
- On-campus guesthouses available at 10% discount for students
- Immersive retreat weekends: Jewelry Boot Camp, Woodlanders Gathering
- Founded 2004; creative placemaking model in a small artist town

**Eureka Springs School of the Arts (ESSA)** — Eureka Springs, AR (pop. ~2,100)
- Year-round workshops, $95+ tuition (no lodging/meals)
- $95 non-refundable deposit per workshop
- Forested campus 5 miles from town
- Nonprofit; ~30 scholarships/year

**Ox-Bow School of Art** — Saugatuck, MI (pop. ~900)
- Affiliated with School of the Art Institute of Chicago
- Michigan Artists Retreat: $600 for 3 nights (lodging + meals + studio, no instruction)
- Lakeside campus, strong community focus

**Arrowmont School of Arts and Crafts** — Gatlinburg, TN (pop. ~4,000)
- Weekend on-campus: $811–$931 (single room, includes tuition + housing + meals)
- Material fees additional: $55–$200 depending on medium
- $300 deposit, balance due 30 days before
- Historic campus in a mountain tourism town

## How Everyone Handles Deposits & Payments

At the ~$500 price point, the pattern is simpler than the $1,500+ retreats:

- $100–$200 non-refundable deposit at booking
- Balance due 30–45 days before the event
- Some offer a simple 2-payment plan (deposit + balance)
- Cancellation: refund minus deposit if 30+ days out, no refund closer in

| Program | Deposit | Balance Due | Cancellation |
|---------|---------|-------------|--------------|
| ESSA | $95 | Before workshop | Refundable minus deposit if 30+ days |
| DoArtWorx | $200 | Before retreat | Non-refundable deposit |
| Waycross | Full at registration | — | Varies |
| Arrowmont | $300 | 30 days before | $100–$300 fee by timing |

**For Nicole at ~$500:** A $150 deposit with balance due 30 days before feels right. Low enough that people don't hesitate, high enough to filter out no-shows.

## Group Size Norms

- Intimate: 6–12 people
- Mid-size: 14–20 people
- Large: 20–30+

For Nicole: 8–12 is the sweet spot. Small enough to be personal, large enough to cover costs. At $500/person with 10 guests, that's $5,000 in revenue before expenses — workable for a weekend in a modest venue.

## Industry Context (Just the Relevant Bits)

**Source: Global Wellness Institute, 2025**
- 88% of wellness trips are domestic
- US consumers spend ~$6,300/person/year on wellness
- The market is growing at 7.6% annually through 2029

**Business Benchmarks (Source: industry guides)**
- Healthy profit margin: 20–40% after expenses
- Recommended cost buffer: 10–15%
- One host's finding: attendees' willingness-to-pay was 50% higher than charged (selvaradov.net)

That last point matters. At $500, Nicole is likely *under* what people would pay. That's fine for a first retreat — it builds goodwill and repeat attendees. She can always raise prices once she has testimonials and a waitlist.
