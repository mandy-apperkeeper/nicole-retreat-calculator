# Your Artist's Retreat in Nashville, IN

Here's the pricing landscape and what the research says about making retreats great.

---

## Pricing Landscape

Most domestic art retreats charge **$750–$2,000 per person** depending on length and what's included.

The formula: Add up costs → add margin → divide by minimum guest count.

| Format | What People Actually Charge |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Day workshop (3–4 hrs, no lodging) | $100–$300 |
| Weekend (2–3 nights, all-inclusive) | $775–$1,200 |
| 5+ night immersive | $1,750–$2,500 |

---

## Nashville, IN Advantages

- Established artist colony with built-in cultural credibility
- Brown County's natural beauty — rolling hills, forests, scenic drives
- Spring and fall: mild weather, peak leaf season draws visitors
- Very few structured visual art retreats in the area — open market

---

## The Formula

```
Total Costs + Profit Margin = Target Revenue
Target Revenue ÷ Minimum Guest Count = Price Per Person
```

Always divide by minimum guest count — the fewest you'd run it for. Everyone above that is profit.

---

## What Goes Into Your Costs

**Fixed (same whether you have 8 guests or 15):**
- Venue / studio rental
- Your fee as facilitator (yes, pay yourself)
- Insurance
- Marketing & admin time

**Variable (per guest):**
- Lodging
- Meals
- Art supplies & materials
- Welcome kit or gifts

---

## Key Decisions

1. **Price with confidence.** Retreat attendees expect to invest. Underpricing signals low value.
2. **Require a deposit.** $300–$500, non-refundable. Reduces no-shows dramatically.
3. **Offer room tiers.** Shared vs. private is standard. Spread is usually $400–$500.
4. **Validate first.** 10–15 real conversations with potential attendees before committing.
5. **Buffer 10–15%.** Something always costs more than expected.

---

## Additional Revenue Streams

- Premium supply kits at a markup
- Optional 1-on-1 sessions at a premium rate
- Nashville/Brown County partnerships (gallery visits, local artisan experiences)
- Post-retreat digital content or recordings

## What Makes Retreats Great (From Attendee Research)

These patterns come from real attendees, host retrospectives, and community discussions.

**1. Connections matter more than curriculum.**
Design for togetherness — shared meals, evening hangouts, collaborative moments. The art instruction matters, but it's not the whole thing.

**2. Welcome all levels explicitly.**
Say it in marketing. Say it on day one. The retreats that get raved about create genuine no-judgment space.

**3. Balance structure with breathing room.**
Clear daily rhythm (morning session, afternoon session) with generous unstructured time.

**4. Venue atmosphere > amenities.**
Natural light, gardens, quiet, beauty. Noise can ruin the experience.

**5. Keep it small.**
8–16 people. Everyone knows names by day two.

**6. First hour sets the tone.**
Personal greeting. Space ready. Food/drink available immediately.

**7. Feed people well.**
Thoughtful > fancy. Include a few small delights.

**8. End with momentum.**
Closing circle, take-home project, plan for staying connected.

**9. Your energy sets the room.**
Build in margin for yourself too.
